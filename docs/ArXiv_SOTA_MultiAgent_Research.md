#### 1. [Pairing AIS with RF: Analysis of a Markovian Approach for Maritime Data Fusion](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18563v1)
- **Authors**: Daniel Grimm, Kyra Jongman, Renato Cordeiro Ferreira
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:59:43Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18563v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > Monitoring maritime activities is important for safety and security, particularly for detecting illegal activities, but remains challenging due to the size and complexity of the maritime domain. The Automatic Identification System (AIS) is commonly used for this purpose, despite its known vulnerabilities. In contrast, space-based radio frequency (RF) observations provide an independent source of vessel activity but lack ship identification and therefore cannot identify vessels on their own. Following the CRISP-DM process, this study applies a Pair Hidden Markov Model (PHMM) to align individual RF detections with AIS trajectories, using temporal and spatial information while modeling noise and timing discrepancies. We evaluate it on NOAA AIS data with synthetic RF detections against nearest-neighbor baselines and analyze its errors with bias tests on trajectory length, sampling density, stationarity and AIS--RF distance. The length-corrected Forward score reaches a top-1 precision of $0.7776$ on the validation split of multi-candidate detections, where errors are possible, and an AUC of $0.637$ for detecting dark vessels among the detections that pass the prefilter. With a single RF detection, the score mostly reflects the AIS trajectory itself, and its errors favor longer and more stationary trajectories. A log-odds score, which compares the full model with a null model without match transitions, raises precision to $0.8580$ and AUC to $0.852$. Its length-corrected variant no longer shows a significant bias towards stationary trajectories and short inter-message distances, but still favors candidates farther from the RF detection. A time-weighted nearest-neighbor baseline without fitting remains ahead (precision $0.9013$, AUC $0.904$), so this single-detection, hand-designed PHMM formulation approaches but does not beat it.

#### 2. [FETTUCCINE: Fast and efficient brain-to-text decoding on mobile devices](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18561v1)
- **Authors**: Jonathan D. McCart, Pranav I. Deevi, Mehdi Azabou
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:59:02Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18561v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > Recent advances in speech neuroprostheses have enabled new communication avenues for people who have lost the ability to speak due to neurological illness or injury. These systems can decode neural activity during attempted speech into text by relying on language priors to achieve high decoding accuracy. This performance currently comes at the cost of using a compute-intensive language modeling stack, presenting a major bottleneck for real-world use. Further, current language models incur highly variable and disruptive inference latencies, limiting communication throughput and preventing users from reliably participating in natural conversation. We introduce FETTUCCINE, an end-to-end brain-to-text framework that leverages the speed and portability of automatic speech recognition (ASR) models to address the aforementioned practical challenges, while achieving usable (<10%) word error rates (WER), efficient adaptation to neural non-stationarities, and reliable performance on mobile devices. When evaluated using publicly available data from an intracortical speech neuroprosthesis user, FETTUCCINE outperforms previous end-to-end methods, achieving as low as 4.39% WER. Most notably, our models successfully run on a commercially-available mobile device with throughput reaching up to 370x real-time, providing the first demonstration of brain-to-text decoding on a mobile device. We also show that our models can be successfully finetuned to extend usable performance to future days. Beyond performance, our approach enables localization of the neural features most relevant for decoding via gradient-based saliency maps, which we show align with well-established physiological priors across different ASR model families. Taken together, our results show that FETTUCCINE overcomes core infrastructural and computational barriers, yielding a new class of portable brain-to-text communication.

#### 3. [Sharp nonuniqueness for the Vlasov--Navier--Stokes system](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18554v1)
- **Authors**: Xingyu Li
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:58:24Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18554v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > We consider the Vlasov--Navier--Stokes system on $\mathbb T^d\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge2$. For smooth data and every $p<2$, $q<\infty$, we construct infinitely many weak solutions with velocity in $L^p_tL^\infty\cap L^1_tW^{1,q}$ and bounded, renormalized distribution function; infinitely many of them violate the energy inequality. For smooth velocity data, weak solutions with velocity in $L^2_tL^\infty$ are unique, so the result is sharp at the endpoint of the Ladyzhenskaya--Prodi--Serrin scale. The fluid carries the nonuniqueness, through the convex integration scheme of Cheskidov and Luo, and the kinetic equation is solved exactly. The main new difficulty is the memory of the particles: the kinetic state at time $t$ depends on the whole past of the velocity, so a modification on a short interval changes the system at all later times. We overcome it by a causal gluing, carried out from left to right in time, which re-solves the system on every interval of regularity. This yields wild solutions that are strong outside a closed set of times of arbitrarily small Hausdorff dimension. A causal mollification extends the construction to rough data, including every finite-energy velocity datum in dimensions two and three; in two dimensions the energy class is thus exactly the borderline of uniqueness. Finally, in three dimensions we construct Leray solutions satisfying the strong energy inequality and show that they are strong outside a closed set of times of vanishing $\mathcal H^{1/2}$ measure.

#### 4. [From smoothed analysis to backward error: The power of adaptive perturbations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18545v1)
- **Authors**: Michał Dereziński, Michael W. Mahoney, Raphael A. Meyer
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:50:40Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18545v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > Smoothed analysis has become a central tool in theoretical computer science, often allowing for significant improvements to worst-case running times if we settle for a solution to a small random perturbation of the problem instance. This is closely related to the classical concept of backward error in numerical analysis, which allows an algorithm to return a solution to an arbitrary perturbation of the problem.   Despite the qualitative similarity of these concepts, we show that they can exhibit large quantitative differences. Notably, we prove that, while a linear system with any $n \times n$ matrix $A$ can be solved to backward error $ε$ using $O(\log(n) / ε)$ matrix-vector products with $A$, any algorithm that solves a random $ε$-sized perturbation of $A$ requires $Ω(n)$ matrix-vector products. This means that a useful perturbation for solving a linear system in backward error must be adaptive to the input. Interestingly, however, randomness is still essential: we show that any deterministic method that attains small backward error for all linear systems provably requires $Ω(n)$ matrix-vector products, resulting in the first separation between randomized and deterministic algorithms in a natural computational model for solving linear systems. Finally, we show that matrix structure can significantly affect backward error complexity. For example, we show a deterministic $O(1/ε)$ matrix-vector product algorithm for symmetric linear systems.

#### 5. [Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18543v1)
- **Authors**: Tony H. Zhang, Connor Mattson, Daniel S. Brown
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:50:05Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18543v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is non-trivial as it is difficult and often impossible for a single person to teleoperate multiple agents simultaneously. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) addresses this by letting a single demonstrator control one agent at a time while the others execute their current policies. However, the resulting imitation policies are limited by the quality of the demonstrations. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), a framework that extends Imitation Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning (IBRL) to cooperative teams and uses R2BC to train the imitation policies. At each time step, every agent receives one action proposal from its imitation policy and one from its RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects among the per-agent combinations of the two. A gated imitation penalty, a soft Q-filtered behavior cloning loss, regularizes each RL policy toward its imitation policy only where the critics value the imitation action more, so that after training each agent executes its RL policy alone, without the centralized critics. On four cooperative multi-agent RL benchmarks, IBMARL surpasses its imitation policies $2.6$-$4.2\times$ faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks, is the only method to surpass it on the other two, and exceeds the demonstrator itself on two of the four. With real human demonstrations IBMARL still surpasses imitation learning alone, and does so more than twice as fast as the best baseline.

#### 7. [Why Finite Intelligence Works in an Infinite World: A Mathematical Theory](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18538v1)
- **Authors**: Mojtaba Soltanalian
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:47:24Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18538v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > An information system often has to learn before it knows what it will be asked. A finite representation can succeed without recording an infinite environment in full when those asks share collective structure, rather than demand unrelated facts scattered arbitrarily across it. We formalize this phenomenon through future-task information, the minimum retained information needed to preserve decision value relative to the available evidence. A joint decision-regret state records posterior comparisons among actions for every permitted task. Under compactness assumptions, every retained representation admits a calibrated replacement based on this state, preserving all task regrets without increasing information; the resulting information-regret curve equals an operational coding rate. Two exact boundaries govern finite preservation: weak-star closure for a fixed action count, and the singular mass of the source-evidence law when all finite action menus are admitted. Collective structure determines information requirements beyond individual-question difficulty. Families with identical source laws, pairwise task metrics, and individual-task rates can have different high-resolution exponents. Covariance-identical coordinate and parity families have different exact rate-distortion curves, with optimal parity retention characterized by a sharp Fourier-entropy inequality. For balanced nested task families, refinement sizes and weighted reach determine the rate within an additive constant, attained by prefix coding under explicit decay conditions. Nonlinear learning and computational separations distinguish preservation from efficient discovery and use. The proposed mathematical theory identifies which shared decision requirements allow finite information to remain useful for infinitely many possible purposes.

#### 8. [Impact of Grid-Forming Inverter Placement and Penetration on Local and Aggregate Frequency](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18534v1)
- **Authors**: Fatma Alfailkawi, Marena Trujillo, Bri-Mathias Hodge
- **Published Date**: 2026-11-13T18:46:18Z
- **arXiv Link**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18534v1
- **Abstract & Key Insights**:
  > Modern power systems are undergoing a huge shift in generation mix, including inverter-based resources that are fundamentally changing the stability dynamics of the grid. Thus, synchronous Generators (SG) are gradually being displaced by Grid-forming inverters (GFM) that are proposed as a solution to continue supporting grid stability. However, the effect of GFM placement in the network on frequency performance and how well aggregate-frequency assessment captures placement sensitivity observed in local frequency metrics remains unclear. This paper evaluates all SG and droop-controlled GFM assignments in the IEEE 39-bus system. A reduced-order model is used for the exhaustive search with load disturbances applied separately at every eligible load bus while considering nadir and Rate of Change of Frequency (RoCoF) derived from both device rating weighted aggregate frequency and local bus frequencies. Results reveal that at fixed GFM penetration, placement produced up to 104 mHz variation in worst-case local nadir and 0.21 Hz/s in maximum local RoCoF. Aggregate-frequency assessment reduced the apparent across placements by 89\% for nadir and 88\% for RoCoF at 50\% GFM penetration. These results indicate that aggregate-frequency assessment alone may obscure favorable or adverse placement effects on local frequency performance.
