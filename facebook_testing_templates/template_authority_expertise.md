# Persuasive Message Template: Authority & Expertise Approach

## Template Overview
This template establishes credibility and trust by positioning the sender as an authority or expert in the field.

## Message Template
```
🎓 As a [credential/authority] with [X] years helping [audience] achieve [result], I've seen what works.

The #1 mistake [audience] make when trying to [goal]: [Common misconception].

Here's what actually works instead:
✅ [Proven strategy/tip 1]
✅ [Proven strategy/tip 2] 
✅ [Proven strategy/tip 3]

Want the complete [resource type] that shows you exactly how to [desired outcome]?
👉 [Link to resource]

#[Relevant hashtag 1] #[Relevant hashtag 2] #[Expertise hashtag]
```

## Customization Guidelines
- Replace `[credential/authority]` with legitimate title/experience (e.g., "certified nutritionist", "Google Ads specialist")
- `[X]` should be accurate years of experience
- `[audience]` is your target demographic
- `[result]` is the specific outcome you help them achieve
- The "[#1 mistake]" should be a genuine common error in your field
- Strategies/tips should be actionable and valuable (not just teasers)
- `[resource type]` could be "guide", "checklist", "video training", etc.
- Link must deliver what's promised in the message
- Use 1-2 expertise-related hashtags (e.g., #ExpertTips, #ProfessionalAdvice)

## Ethical Considerations
- Credentials and experience claims must be truthful and verifiable
- Avoid claiming expertise you don't possess
- The "mistake" should be genuinely common, not fabricated to sell
- Strategies shared should be legitimate and safe
- Resource must provide substantial value, not just be a sales pitch
- Comply with Facebook's policies on making expert claims

## A/B Testing Variables
- Test different credentials/experience levels
- Experiment with different common mistakes/pain points
- Vary the number and type of strategies shared (2 vs 3 vs 4)
- Test different resource formats (guide vs checklist vs video)
- Experiment with CTA wording around the resource
